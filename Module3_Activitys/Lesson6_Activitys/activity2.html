Space is a vast, mostly invisible universe filled with stars, planets, galaxies, and mysterious phenomena that continue to intrigue scientists. The Universe and Its Scale Space begins beyond Earth's atmosphere, commonly defined at the Kármán line, 100 km above sea level, and is a near-perfect vacuum with temperatures around −270.45 °C (−454.81 °F) where sound cannot travel due to the lack of molecules. The observable universe is about 13.8 billion years old and contains an estimated 2 trillion galaxies, each with hundreds of billions of stars. Most of the universe is invisible, composed of dark matter and dark energy, with ordinary matter making up less than 5% of the total mass-energy content. Space Facts +2 Solar System Highlights Our solar system is approximately 4.57 billion years old. Mercury and Venus are the only planets without moons, while some moons, like Ganymede and Titan, are larger than Mercury. Venus is the hottest planet due to its thick atmosphere and greenhouse effect, with surface temperatures around 462 °C (864 °F),. Enceladus, a moon of Saturn, reflects 90% of sunlight, keeping its surface extremely cold at −201 °C (−394 °F),. The Fact Site +1 Stars and Cosmic Phenomena The Milky Way alone contains 100–400 billion stars. Stars can be torn apart if they pass too close to black holes, and astronomers have observed gravitational redshift effects as stars orbit these massive objects. Fast Radio Bursts (FRBs) are brief, powerful flashes of radio waves from distant galaxies, possibly linked to magnetars, though their exact origin remains uncertain. Space Facts +2 Earth and Its Moon Earth is the only known planet to host life, protected by its atmosphere and magnetic field from harmful solar radiation. The Moon is slowly drifting away at about 3.8 cm per year, which will eventually prevent total solar eclipses. A day on Earth is slightly less than 24 hours, lasting 23 hours, 56 minutes, and 4 seconds. James Webb Discovery Space Exploration The International Space Station is the largest crewed object in space, and spacecraft have visited all known planets in our solar system. Sunlight takes about 8 minutes to reach Earth, and the Sun loses 4 million tons of mass every second through nuclear fusion. In roughly 5 billion years, the Sun will expand into a red giant, potentially engulfing Mercury, Venus, and possibly Earth. Space Facts +2 These facts illustrate the immense scale, complexity, and mystery of space, highlighting both what we know and the vast unknowns that continue to inspire exploration and discovery.

Cars are complex machines with a rich history, global impact, and fascinating technological and cultural facts. Historical Milestones The modern car is generally considered to have been born in 1886 when German inventor Carl Benz built the Benz Patent-Motorwagen, a one-cylinder, four-stroke engine vehicle capable of 16 km/h. The Ford Model T, introduced in 1908, became the first mass-produced car, making automobiles accessible to the general public and revolutionizing manufacturing with the assembly line. Early innovations also include the first self-propelled vehicle, the Cugnot Fardier in 1769, and the first effective windshield wiper invented by Mary Anderson in 1903. The Fact File +1 Production and Popularity As of 2024, there are approximately 1.475 billion cars worldwide, with the United States leading at 300 million vehicles, followed by China with 78 million. The Toyota Corolla is the best-selling car globally, with over 44.1 million units sold since 1966, averaging one car sold every 15 seconds. The Volkswagen Beetle was the most popular car of the 20th century, selling over 20 million units. FactRetriever +1 Technology and Design A typical car contains over 30,000 parts, and about 80% of a car is recyclable. Modern vehicles include sophisticated electronics, with the average car containing more than 100 miles of code in its internal computer systems. Safety features like airbags deploy at speeds up to 4,500 MPH within 40 milliseconds, designed to protect occupants in collisions. The iconic BMW logo represents a spinning propeller, reflecting the company’s origins in aviation.
