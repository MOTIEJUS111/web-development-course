Space

Galaxies are vast systems of stars, gas, and dust, all bound together by gravity. The Milky Way is a spiral galaxy with a disk of stars spanning more than 100,000 light-years. The Andromeda Galaxy is the largest and brightest of the galaxies in Earth's region, and the Magellanic Clouds are two dwarf galaxies close to the Milky Way. Galaxies can organize into groups of about 100 or fewer members held together by their mutual gravity, forming larger structures called clusters. Superclusters are not gravitationally bound and can be empty voids, walls of galaxies, and other large-scale structures that make up the cosmic web of matter in the universe. Space Facts

Moon

Space facts about earths moon The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite and plays a crucial role in shaping our planet's history and the dynamics of our solar system. Here are some interesting space facts about the Moon: The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite and is tidally locked to Earth, meaning it always shows the same side to us. 1 It has a very thin and tenuous atmosphere called an exosphere, which is not breathable. 1 The Moon has no moons, rings, or rings of dust. 1 It is the only celestial body beyond Earth that has been visited by human beings. 1 The Moon's surface is covered in craters, maria, and highlands, and it has a solid, rocky surface cratered and pitted from impacts. 1 The Moon's surface temperature ranges from -173 °C to +127 °C. 1 The Moon formed about 4.5 billion years ago and is the largest and most massive satellite relative to its planet in the inner Solar System. 1 The Moon's mean diameter is about 3,475 km (≈2,159 miles), and its mass is approximately 7.35 × 10²² kg (about 1.23% of Earth’s mass). 1 The Moon's surface gravity is about 1.62 m/s², roughly 1/6 that of Earth. 1 The Moon is drifting away from Earth, moving approximately 3.8 cm away from our planet every year. 1 These facts highlight the Moon's significance in our solar system and its role in our understanding of space exploration and planetary science.
